The detail

Your rules (1 Oct): never two levels from one match, and after level 4 a match should almost never pay for a level, with 60→61 far longer than 4→5. Measured by playing 60 careers through the real game code. Matches per level for a rising player: 4→5 = 2, 5→6 = 5, 20→21 ≈ 6, 40→41 ≈ 9, 60→61 ≈ 27 (today 2 / 2.6 / 1.4 / 7 / 9). A single match paying for a whole level after level 4: 1.0–1.6% of matches → 0.2–0.3%. Biggest one-match rise: 12 levels → 1. Where a 12-season career ends: rising player 80 → 65, star 90 → 73; about half of star careers reach 90 within 20 seasons. Your earlier anchors moved: levels 11→20 now cost 304,000 points (you said about 100,000 — at that price a star still gained a level every match or two in the teens); 81→90 cost 12.7 million (about 10 million). Premier League debut bonus 18,000 → 6,000. The chart in More pictures shows today and all three options. (measured)
