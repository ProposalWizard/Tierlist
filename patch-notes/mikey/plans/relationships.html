PLAN

Relationships revamp

For Mikey to review · 4 October 2026 · read from the code and measured in simulated careers; nothing is built yet

In one line: today the bars fill to 100 within about 20 matches for an average player, so the relationship games barely matter. The plan gives each bar one clear job, makes the bars move slowly and sensibly, and gives each relationship its own game.

2 of 5Bars with a big job in the game (boss, team)

Decisions for you

1. The Sponsors bar
Since the Sponsors revamp, each brand has its own happiness, and that is what decides renewals. The Sponsors bar on Relations changes nothing any more. My pick: remove it, and turn the Sponsor Event game into a game that raises one brand's happiness, on the Sponsors screen. Or: keep it as the average of your brands' happiness.
2. Happiness ("You")
Today it only matters for 2 sponsor brands (they want 45 or more). My pick: give it a real job: low happiness gives you less energy back when you rest; high happiness gives a little more. Or: remove it.
3. Should bars slowly drift back towards the middle?
Without it, a bar you never look after stays high forever. My pick: yes, slowly (above 60 it eases down a little each match, below 40 it eases up), so you have a reason to keep a relationship going.
4. The four new games
See "The new games" below. Say yes, no, or change each one.

What's wrong with it

  • The bars fill to 100 almost straight away
    Median of 200 simulated careers each, using the game's own per-match table; level of each bar after that many matches, starting at 50. Played-out careers through the real season code gave the same picture: an average player had all three at 100 by match 11–24. measured
    Player (average rating)After 10After 20After 46 (a season)
    Struggling (5.9)B48 T57 F54B46 T63 F59B43 T81 F76
    Average (6.7)B75 T74 F97B100 T100 F100B100 T100 F100
    Good (7.4)B100 T90 F100B100 T100 F100B100 T100 F100
    Why: a 7.0 rating already pays +3/+2/+4 a match, goals and star man add more, and nothing ever brings a bar back down. Even a struggling player gets team and fans near 100 in two seasons.
  • The games are not worth playing
    Once a bar is over 60, a win pays +1 and a loss costs −4: you have to win 4 games in 5 just to stay level. A single 7.0 match pays more than three won games. And because the bars are full after ~20 matches, there is nothing left to raise.
  • Every relationship is the same game
    Matching emoji pairs has nothing to do with your manager, your team-mates or the fans.
  • Two bars do little or nothing, and two "?" lines aren't true
    The Sponsors bar does nothing (decision 1). Happiness only matters to two brands (decision 2). The Fans "?" promises fan mail; the Sponsors "?" says the bar keeps them paying.

The plan

  • A. One clear job per bar, and a true "?" line
    BarIts job
    BossWhether you play. Benched when it's low, captain-ready when it's high. (As now.)
    TeamHow often team-mates pass to you, and how well your passes get finished. (As now.)
    FansYour home-match boost, sponsor brands that want popular players, and the tone of the social feed. (As now, with the "?" fixed.)
    YouDecision 2: how much energy Rest gives back.
    SponsorsDecision 1: removed from Relations; each brand's happiness does the job.
    On each bar, small marks show where something changes (benched below here, captain above here), with no numbers, as you asked for the fame levels.
  • B. Bars that move slowly and make sense
    Three rules:
    • Centred on a fair rating. A match rated about 6.3 moves nothing; better ratings raise the bars, worse ones lower them, in proportion.
    • Harder near the top. The closer a bar is to 100, the less a good match adds; the closer to 0, the less a bad one takes.
    • Slow drift (decision 3): above 60 a bar eases down a little each match; below 40 it eases up.

  • C. The new games: one per relationship
    RelationshipGameHow it plays
    BossTalk to your managerNot a reflex game: a short chat. He raises something from your real situation (you were benched, you scored twice, a new position). You pick one of three replies. His style (trusts his players, or rotates) decides which reply lands.
    TeamTraining-ground challengeA crossbar challenge or a rondo with team-mates, played with the real ball and kick (built on the one match engine, with a "you and team-mates" scene).
    FansSigning sessionFans queue at the barrier holding a shirt, a ball or a photo. Tap the right one and sign before each fan gives up. More fans come the more famous you are.
    A sponsorShoot an advertOn the Sponsors screen, for one brand. A timing game: hit your marks when the director calls. Raises that brand's happiness, which decides your renewal.
    YouDay offPick how to spend it: the garden, golf, a trip. Better options cost money or use things you own. No way to fail.
    What a game pays: more when the bar is low and less when it's high (Harry's rule: "the higher up you go, the harder it is"). For example +6 below 40, +4 from 40 to 70, +2 above 70, +1 sometimes above 85. A loss costs −1 (the boss chat −2). Each game once a week, using one of your 3 actions. I'll measure these numbers on the new bars before building.
  • D. Fix now, whatever you decide
    The Fans "?" line (no fan mail) and the Sponsors "?" line.

Order of work

Part 1Bars that move sensibly (B), one job each with true "?" lines (A, D), and your decisions on Sponsors and happiness. Small, and every career feels it at once. Part 2The new games, one at a time: the boss chat first (it matters most, since boss decides whether you play), then team, fans, the advert, the day off. LaterTie-ins with the manager and transfers revamps: a manager who talks to you, and team and fans that affect whether clubs want you.
