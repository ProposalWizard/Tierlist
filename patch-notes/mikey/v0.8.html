The headline: brands come to you

  • Sponsors rebuilt: offers, deals, slots and weekly money

    Problem: the Sponsors page was a list of ten categories you signed yourself, paid once a season. Nothing arrived, there was nothing to decide, and nothing could go wrong.

    Why: it was built as a checklist, not as brands with their own terms.

    Fix: brands now send you offers. Each one is a brand (invented, like STRYDE boots or VOLTA Energy), a weekly fee, 1 to 3 seasons, one or two targets, and sometimes a clause. You have deal slots: 1 at Local Name up to 5 at Icon, and one deal per kind of brand. An offer lasts 2 weeks. Signing pays two weeks' fee straight away. Deals belong to you, not the club, so they carry on if you move. screens seen; money measured

  • Who gets offers

    Only a player who is playing and playing well: in the squad, with recent ratings of 6.2 or better. More fame and better form bring more offers, and your star rating nudges the money up a little. Each kind of brand also wants something that fits it: goals for boots, appearances for a drinks brand, a trophy for electronics, a car of your own for a watch brand, a title-chasing club for a car brand. measured

  • Targets only ever add

    Targets vary: score 14 goals this season, score in 4 appearances in a row, win Player of the Month, average 7.2, get promoted, win a trophy. Hitting one pays a bonus and makes the brand happier. Missing one costs no money, only a little of the brand's happiness. measured

  • Brand happiness decides the renewal

    Each deal shows the brand's face and a bar. Good ratings, targets and trophies push it up; misses and scandals pull it down. When the deal ends, a happy brand offers a renewal with a 15–30% raise, a brand on the fence offers a raise, the same money or a cut, and an unhappy one walks. measured

  • Things that happen

    Bidding war: two brands of the same kind want you; sign one and the other walks. Buy-out: a rival offers more money and pays to take you from your current brand; you can ask your brand to match it (a happy one usually does). Scandal: being caught for corruption, or a heavy night at the casino (losing more than 8 weeks' wages in one visit, 1 time in 4), ends any deal with a behaviour clause and upsets the rest. One-off adverts: cash for your first goal, first hat-trick, 50 and 100 goals, first Player of the Month, first trophy and first cap, without using a slot. Home nation: a national brand once you win a cap. measured

  • Negotiating and walking away

    "More money" opens the same two-desks negotiation screen as transfers; push too hard and the brand leaves. "Longer deal" and "Easier target" can each be asked once. Leaving a deal early costs its whole guaranteed value: every week it promised. measured

Changed

  • Sponsor money is now weekly, on the after-match card

    Before: once-a-season fees, plus "image rights" every match from the sponsors relationship bar. Now: your deals' weekly fees, paid with your wage on the weekend match, and paid even in a week you don't play. The per-match image-rights money is gone. measured

    Headphones and a football video game join boots, sports drink, food, sports and casual clothing, electronics, cosmetics, watch, jewellery, car and home nation.

Known issues

  • Not played through on a screen LOW
    The Sponsors screens were seen with example deals. Offers arriving during a real career, the negotiation screen for a sponsor, and the after-match line were checked by tests and a played-through test career, not by eye.
  • Offers are not a phone pop-up yet LOW
    They show as a badge on the Phone and in Sponsors → Offers. A pop-up was left for later, as asked.
  • The old sponsors relationship bar LOW
    Relations still shows a Sponsors bar and minigame. It no longer changes your money; brand happiness now does that job per deal.
