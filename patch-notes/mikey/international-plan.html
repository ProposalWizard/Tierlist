Build every competition the real way, simulate all of it, and store full detail only for your own country.

Every nation and every qualifying match is played in the background each season. You play only your own country's matches, in the real match engine. For every other competition, the save keeps the winner, the runner-up and the final score.

What the game has today (Leo, 11–12 Oct)

  • Four international breaks a season, one match each
    September, October, November and March. The match is called a qualifier, but there are no qualifying groups or tables behind it.
  • A call-up roll at each break
    A chance (2%–97%) from your rating against your country's level, your last 3 match ratings, and whether you were in the last squad. There is no relationship with the national manager.
  • A summer tournament every other year
    Euros (24 teams) and World Cup (32 teams). Who qualifies is decided by team strength plus luck, with no qualifying matches. The real World Cup now has 48 teams.
  • Club games carry on during breaks
    The England match goes on a midweek date and your club still plays at the weekend. In real life the Premier League and the Championship stop.
  • Your national squad = the best-rated players of your nationality
    Rating only. The division they play in doesn't count, so an 83-rated player in League One would still get in.
  • Team strength is set for about 60 countries
    Every other country gets a default strength of 60.

Europe
12 groups (six of 4, six of 5), home and away
  • The 12 group winners qualify.
  • Play-offs: the 12 runners-up plus the 4 best Nations League group winners that didn't finish top two go into 4 paths of 4 teams.
  • Each path is a one-match semi-final (seeded team at home) and a one-match final. The 4 path winners qualify.
South America
One league of all 10 teams, home and away (18 matchdays over 3 years)
  • 1st–6th qualify. 7th goes to the inter-continental play-off.
Africa
9 groups of 6, home and away
  • The 9 group winners qualify.
  • The 4 best runners-up play off; the winner goes to the inter-continental play-off.
Asia
Five rounds
  • Round 1: the 20 lowest-ranked teams play two-legged ties; 10 winners go on.
  • Round 2: 36 teams in 9 groups of 4; the top 2 of each group go on (and also reach the Asian Cup).
  • Round 3: 18 teams in 3 groups of 6; the top 2 of each group qualify (6 places).
  • Round 4: the 3rd- and 4th-placed teams form 2 groups of 3 (one venue); the 2 winners qualify.
  • Round 5: the two runners-up play home and away; the winner goes to the inter-continental play-off.
North & Central America, Caribbean
Three rounds (hosts skip it)
  • Round 1: the 4 lowest-ranked teams play two-legged ties.
  • Round 2: 30 teams in 6 groups of 5 (each team plays 2 home, 2 away); the top 2 of each group go on.
  • Round 3: 12 teams in 3 groups of 4, home and away. The 3 winners qualify; the 2 best runners-up go to the inter-continental play-off.
Oceania
Three rounds
  • Round 1: the 4 lowest-ranked teams in a mini knockout.
  • Round 2: 8 teams in 2 groups of 4; the top 2 of each group go on.
  • Round 3: semi-finals and a final. The winner qualifies; the runner-up goes to the inter-continental play-off.
Inter-continental play-off
6 teams → 2 places, one-off matches at one venue
  • The 2 highest in the FIFA ranking go straight to the two finals.
  • The other 4 play two semi-finals. Extra time, then penalties.
How nations reach the other tournaments
  • Euros (2028 rules): 12 groups of 4 or 5, home and away, 10 matchdays in 2027. The 12 winners and the 8 best runners-up qualify (20). The other places go through March play-offs (the 4 other runners-up and Nations League teams). The 4 hosts (England, Scotland, Wales, Republic of Ireland) play the qualifiers; 2 places are held back for hosts who don't qualify by right.
  • Copa América (2024): the 10 South American teams qualify automatically. The 6 CONCACAF teams came from the CONCACAF Nations League (4 quarter-final winners + 2 play-off winners).
  • Africa Cup of Nations (2025): the 8 lowest-ranked teams play a two-legged preliminary round. Then 48 teams in 12 groups of 4, home and away; the top 2 qualify (24). The host plays in a group too but is already in.
  • Asian Cup (2027): built into World Cup qualifying. The 18 teams from World Cup Round 2 qualify, plus the host. The other teams play a third round of 6 groups of 4; the 6 group winners qualify.
  • Gold Cup (2025): 8 places from the CONCACAF Nations League, 7 from a March preliminary round, and 1 invited guest.
  • OFC Nations Cup (2024): the lowest-ranked teams play a qualifying round; the winner joins the finals.
What changes after 2028 (not confirmed)
  • World Cup 2030: Morocco, Portugal and Spain host; Argentina, Paraguay and Uruguay each host one opening match. All 6 qualify automatically. Number of teams: CONMEBOL announced 64 on 20 Jul 2026, but FIFA has not confirmed it. Slots per continent: not confirmed.
  • European qualifying from 2028 onwards: UEFA announced (20 May 2026) a two-tier system: League 1 (36 teams, 3 groups of 12, 6 matches each) and League 2 (the rest). The top team in each League 1 group qualifies; the rest go to play-offs. Final approval was due on 15 Sep 2026. Not confirmed by me.
  • Nations League from 2028/29: 3 leagues of 18 teams (3 groups of 6). Same announcement.
  • Africa: an African Nations League every Sep–Nov from 2029 (reported; first year differs between sources).

Club football during a break
Which divisions stop
  • Premier League and Championship: no league games on break weekends. The fixture list needs these weekends left empty.
  • League One and League Two: keep playing. A club with 3 or more players called up can ask to postpone. In the game: if you are called up, you miss that club game.
  • National League and below: keep playing. A call-up from there is very rare, but if it happens you miss the club game.
  • Asian Cup (January), and an Africa Cup played in winter: a called-up player misses about 3–5 weeks of club games.

What the bar decides
The same lines as your club manager
  • Under 55: not called up.
  • 55 or more: in the squad. 55 is the same standing your club manager needs to start you.
  • In the squad: you start if your national standing is the best among the players in your position. Otherwise you are on the bench and come on later, using the club's substitute ladder (on at 80, 70, 60 or 50 minutes).

The bar replaces today's call-up roll. A small country is easier: its bar starts higher, and the bar it needs is lower.

Picking the national squad

Each player's score
Rating, adjusted for the division he plays in
  • Score = overall rating + a division adjustment + 2 if he starts for his club.
  • Division adjustment: Premier League and the top leagues abroad 0, Championship −4, League One −8, League Two −11, National League −14.
  • Example: an 83-rated League One player scores 75, so an 80-rated Premier League player is picked ahead of him.
  • No goals or assists count. You were right that defenders and keepers would lose out.
The squad
23 for a break, 26 for a tournament (at least 3 goalkeepers)
  • Picked by position to fill the formation twice over, plus a third goalkeeper.
  • If a country doesn't have enough real players, made-up players fill the gaps, as now.
