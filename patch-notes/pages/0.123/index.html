FIXED AFTER YOUR SECOND PLAYTEST (28 OCT)

Ball frozen in the air after a dive that didn’t catch itseen

Why: when a save ends the chance, the match stops the ball where he touched it, often in mid-air. Fix (corrected 28 Oct): if he was the last to touch it, it goes into his hands and stays there through the landing and getting up. It never flies away. A miss that stops in mid-air (off the pitch, wide) carries on its own way. Checked: 21 real catches over about 150 shots, 0 frames with the ball outside his hands, and 0 balls left frozen in the air.

FIXED AFTER YOUR PLAYTEST (28 OCT)

The ball stayed frozen in the air, nowhere near himseen

Why: the new keeper only took over when it had planned his move from the shot. Other catches fell back to the old drawing, which leaves the ball where the match parks it, half a metre up at the spot it met him. That covered a cross he claims, a ball he palms then falls on, a ball that arrived early, and the first catch before his new drawings had loaded. On the catch frame the ball was also drawn twice, and the white flash sat beside him.

Fix: every frame, if the match says he has the ball, it goes into his hands. With no move planned he gathers it where he stands. Still down after a palm, he gets up with it. If his drawing hasn’t loaded, the ball sits at his chest, never in the air. The ball is drawn once, straight after him, and the flash is at his gloves. In the 3D view the ball sits between the 3D keeper’s hands.

Checked: a new checker plays real chances without filming and measures, every frame, where the ball is drawn. After the fix: 31 real catches over 992 shots, 0 frames with the ball anywhere but his hands, in all three looks (catches are rare: the match holds about 1 shot in 30). Pictures of every catch checked by eye.

Not checked: the 3D view. It is too slow on the test machine to reach a catch (2 shots in 25 minutes). It uses the same rule, so please check it on your phone.

WHAT IS BUILT

16 new keeper movesseen

Made from the 3D keeper, like the drawings now: diving catch (high and low, both sides), dive and palm away (both sides), catch at his chest, catch at his feet, get up with the ball, get up without it (both sides), stand holding the ball. 11 to 19 drawings per move at 15 to 22 a second (now 4 to 6 at 9). Every drawing records where the ball sits in his gloves. Checked: no body part goes through another (RUL-3D-005), his size is unchanged (RUL-3D-006), his feet stay on the grass.

The dive starts in timeseen

At the shot, the game works out when and where the ball will reach him (with the same air drag and grass friction as the match), and starts his dive so his gloves get there as the ball does. On the first takes he dived up to 1 s too early on long and rolling shots and lay there waiting; fixed and refilmed (the clips above are after the fix). Two more caught on the diving-catch takes and fixed: the match cut away after a catch while he still lay on the floor, and in B the slow motion never ended after a catch.

Ball in his gloves; land; get upseen

On a catch the ball goes into his gloves and moves with them through the dive, landing, getting up and standing. On a palm away the ball flies off as the match sends it. He lands on the grass, lies a beat (0.45 s), then gets up. The match now waits 3.4 s after a diving save (was 1.8 s) so you see him get up, and 2.4 s after a standing catch.

The plan as written on 26 Oct

1 · THE BALL GOES INTO HIS GLOVES AND STAYS THERE

He catches it, and it moves with himplan

When he catches it, the ball goes into his gloves and moves with his hands through the dive, the landing and getting up. Then he holds it and the play moves on, as now. To do that, every new drawing records where his gloves are, so the game can put the ball exactly there in each frame.

Drawing, from the side. The diving catch, planned: set, push off, ball in his gloves in the air, lands on his side with it tucked in, up onto a knee, stands holding it.

2 · A REAL DIVE: PUSH OFF, FLY, LAND, GET UP

He dives, hits the floor, and gets up properlyplan

The dive becomes one move from start to finish: he pushes off his leg, flies, lands on his side with a bump and a puff off the grass, lies for a moment, then gets up (rolls onto a knee, stands). No more sliding along in one pose, and no snap from lying to walking. The dive starts in time, so his hands reach the ball as it arrives (today the ball is pushed away before he moves), and no second dive after he gets up (3D).

  • Smoother: about 18 drawings per move at 24 a second (now 4 to 6 at 9).
  • Made from the 3D keeper, as the drawings are now, so 2D and 3D match.
  • New moves: diving catch (high and low, both sides), catch at his feet, catch at his chest, dive and palm it away, spill it, get up with the ball, get up without it.

3 · BETTER GRAPHICS: THE KEEPER AND THE BALL

He and the ball look like they belong togetherplan

The ball gets the same light and shade as the players: a soft shadow on the grass that follows it into his hands, the right size next to his gloves, and a small squash when it hits his gloves. His drawings are made at twice the detail (sharper edges), and his shadow stretches as he dives.

4 · THE SAME EVERYWHERE

2D, 3D and goal videos all show itplan

The 2D match, the 3D view (the ball sits in the 3D keeper’s hands) and the goal videos and highlights all use it, because they all draw the same keeper.
