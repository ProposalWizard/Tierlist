2 · How the app works, and how updates work

The app is a frame. The game inside it is the live website. There is still only one game.

  • We build the frame oncefrom the rulesApp icon and name, sign-in, saves kept on the phone, an offline screen instead of a blank page, the back button, portrait lock. This is what Apple checks, because it rejects “a website in a box”.
  • We don’t build a second gameThe match, career and every screen stay on the website. Nothing is copied, so nothing drifts out of step.
  • Built in the cloudnot checkedA service called Codemagic makes the iPhone and Android apps. About 500 free build-minutes a month.

Harry said · feedback video 0:45“…if they sign in with Apple they still then get to sign in with Google and it’s the same account. Okay, cool.”

Updates: two lanes

Instant, no store

  • Matches, physics, keepers, scenarios
  • Every screen, menu and the look
  • New modes that only need the screen
  • Balance, prices, difficulty, player data
  • Bug fixes

Needs a new store version

  • App icon, name, splash screen
  • Sign-in methods, push notifications
  • A new phone permission. The face scan uses the camera, so that permission goes in the first version
  • Vibration, in-app purchases, anything that talks to the phone directly

The catches

  • Old frames: a web change that needs a new frame feature must hide itself until players update. The game checks first.
  • Apple’s rules still cover what the website adds. The app can’t turn into a different kind of app, and can’t sell coins around Apple’s payments.
  • If the website is down, the app is down. An offline screen covers it (section 5).

Rules: from Apple’s and Google’s pages. The face-scan camera use: checked in the code. No app exists yet, so none of this is tested.

Two corrections to the earlier guide

  • Apple no longer names Sign in with Apple as the only optionfrom the rulesRule 4.8 accepts any equivalent login that limits data to name and email, lets people hide their email, and doesn’t track them for ads.
  • Google bans its sign-in page inside an app’s embedded web viewfrom the rulesSo the app must use the phone’s own Google sign-in. The current website sign-in would not work as it is.
What Apple’s rules say about a website in an app
  • 4.2: “Your app should include features, content, and UI that elevate it beyond a repackaged website.” This is why the frame needs real app parts.
  • 4.7: apps may offer web mini apps and games not built in, but “you are responsible for all such software”. The website’s content must follow Apple’s rules too.
  • 2.3.1(a): no “hidden, dormant, or undocumented features”. New features must be described in the review notes.
  • 3.1.1: unlocking anything in the iPhone app must use Apple’s payments. Coins bought this way must not expire.
  • 2.5.2 bans downloading code that “introduces or changes features”. The licence agreement (3.3.1(B)) allows downloading interpreted code like web files if the app keeps its main purpose. Both count as a reading, not an Apple statement.

Harry said · review video 10:24“What’s the difference between career game only inside the app fully offline and frame loading website plus offline screen?”

The difference, plainly

1Frame + offline screen recommended firstThe game still lives on the internet. The app loads it like a browser would. No signal: a friendly “you’re offline” screen, and your save waits on the phone. Small to build.
2Career game fully offline The career game’s files are packed inside the app, so a started career plays with no signal at all. It syncs when signal returns. Bigger to build: a second way of building the site, and login has to be reworked.
3Whole site inside the app Every page in the app. Far too big: 124 server routes and 23 pages would need rewriting. Don’t.

Why New Star Soccer is 13+ and we might not be
  • Its PEGI 12 is oldfrom the rulesPEGI changed the rule in 2020 and said it would not apply it to older games. A new game with casino-style games is PEGI 18 under today’s rules.
  • Its Apple 13+ says “Infrequent Simulated Gambling”from the rulesnot checkedIt has one casino with a few games. Ours is six games in one menu, which reads as more prominent. How Apple’s reviewers would judge ours is unconfirmed.
  • Fake chips don’t change the tierfrom the rulesThe rules ignore what the chips are. Giving XP for wins makes it slightly worse, not better.

  1. Block it on the server, not just hide the buttonThe app adds a marker to the web view. The server then refuses the casino pages and casino calls. A hidden button alone leaves it reachable by address.rules + not checked
  2. Block all six gamesIncluding competition bets and the betting in Goalie Mode. Apple counts sports betting as gambling.from the rules

Harry said · review video 7:40“Hiding casino in the app but keeping it on the website? Hmm, yeah, not worth it.” (He meant a hidden copy, which C isn’t.)

Real-money coins

  • Swap real money for casino coins: don’tfrom the rulesIt can push the rating to 17+ or 18, and UK regulator research names in-game purchases as the strongest sign of kids moving to real gambling. It isn’t illegal if nothing can be cashed out or traded, but it is the riskiest idea.
  • One item priced both ways (10M coins or £5): allowedfrom the rulesThe £5 must go through Apple’s or Google’s payments. The catch: the two prices set a public exchange rate, so casino winnings start to look like real money. The safest version keeps store-bought money and casino coins as two separate wallets.
  • What Apple and Google keepfrom the rulesnot checkedAbout 15% with Apple’s Small Business Program (from Apple’s page). The website’s card payment costs about 1.5% + 20p, not checked for us.

Harry said · feedback video 1:40“Let’s say Ronaldo free kick style costs 10 million in-game coins, but only costs… like say five pound. Would that be allowed if there’s casino in the game?”

The safety checklist, if coins ever cost money
  • Nothing won in the casino can ever be cashed out, traded, gifted or sold.
  • Store-bought currency can never be staked in the casino.
  • No real-money purchase of casino chips or spins, and no “buy more chips” at a zero balance.
  • No random paid items, or show the odds before purchase.
  • Bought currency doesn’t expire and can be restored.
  • Answer the rating questions honestly. Never add the casino to the app later through the website without telling the store.
  • No ads or links to real-money casinos or betting sites.
  • Not in Apple’s Kids Category or Google’s Families programme.

From Apple, Google and UK regulator pages. Not legal advice; a one-hour check with a games lawyer before selling anything is worth it.

The next step that settles it: once both accounts exist, fill in Apple’s and Google’s rating questionnaires twice, with the casino and without. Then we have real ratings, not guesses.
