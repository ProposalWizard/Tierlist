Fixed off the back of the call

  • Delete works on any scenario now, not just one-on-ones
    Leo was blocked: “I added one and I can't delete it”, and Delete looked like a one-on-one-only feature.
    Volley cards
    9 10
    Two causes, neither about one-on-ones
    • The Delete button only appeared once a scenario had been SAVED. The only saved scenarios were one-on-ones, so it looked kind-specific. It is always there now.
    • A generated card had nowhere to be deleted TO — versions come from a count, not a list, so there was no way to say “not this one”. There is now, and it is remembered after a reload.
    • On a saved scenario Delete still clears the database and the code. On a card that was never saved it just takes the card out, and says so, instead of reporting a server error for something that never reached a server.
  • The X is the reject mark, not a delete — it says so now
    Asked twice on the call what it meant: “is that delete, or does that mean something else?” It reads “No good”.
  • The scenario picture on desktop — too small, then too big, now 400×640
    It was hard-coded to phone size on any screen. First pass overshot to 500×800. Phone is unchanged throughout.

Decided on the call

  • Sprint to something releasable, then tune it
    “This could release today, I'm not even joking” — if the scenarios were done, the trial was done, and the engine worked everywhere.
    What that means in practice
    • The three things between here and a release: every chance kind has drawings, the trial works, and the same match engine runs in the trial and in training rather than only in a match.
    • Everything else on this page waits behind those three. The reason is that tuning a chance is guesswork until the chances themselves exist — “it's hard to make the scenario stuff good when it's not done”.
  • A stronger opponent means FEWER chances, not different ones
    Non-league against another non-league side might be ten chances. Against Liverpool it might be two or three. A one-on-one is still one of the things those two can be.
    Where it was left
    • Agreed: the opponent's strength changes how many chances you get.
    • Open: whether two one-on-ones against Liverpool as a non-league side is wrong. Harry's read is that it does not strictly need to make sense, so long as chances are rarer.
    • Also agreed: your position should tilt which chances you get, not decide it. A striker getting only striker chances was called out as wrong — whatever position you play should get you into the game a lot.
  • Extra modes arrive through the game, never through a settings switch
    The home screen already has casino, sponsors, awards, trophies. Adding ownership and garden to that list was called overwhelming.
    The shape agreed, and the one that was rejected
    • Rejected: a Settings toggle that says “turn on funky features”. Called silly, and for a good reason — nobody finds it, so nobody uses it, so why build it.
    • Agreed instead: it arrives as an event. Around £1m, an investor approaches you with a chance to buy into some clubs, and ownership unlocks from that moment.
    • Mikey's version was a profile tab you scroll down to. Same instinct, and worth keeping in mind — the difference is whether it is discovered or given to you.
  • Ownership stays. The son mechanic and the agent game go somewhere else.
    Investing and eventually owning a club is wanted. Spawning a son, ageing him up, running an agency is a different game wearing this one's clothes.
    Why the line is drawn there
    • The test used on the call: would somebody see this and think “what the hell is this?” Investing survives it. A potion that ages up your son does not.
    • The concern is not that the ideas are bad — they were both wanted originally. It is that a game with all of them in reads as a gimmick rather than as a football career.
    • Manager after you retire is fine, and is mostly built already. Agent and the chaotic simulation stuff become their own game, using this as the base.
  • This engine is a base for more than one game
    “We could make the best possible NSS, then the best possible other game.” Anything cut from here is not thrown away, it is the start of the next one.
  • Scenario work is split by chance kind, and everyone saves into the same place
    Pick a kind, make ten to fifteen varied ones, save each as you go, then commit the whole lot at once from Tuning & Commit.
    The bit that was unclear on the call
    • Do NOT press commit per scenario. Save them, then commit once — that is the whole reason the Tuning & Commit page exists.
    • A kind with no drawings has no rule set, so the first ten for a new kind are what teach it what that chance IS. Range matters more than polish.
    • Tune is not the same as Save. Tune records a correction and changes nothing on its own; it waits until several corrections agree before proposing anything.

Ideas from the call — none of these are started

  • Chances should come out of the passage of play
    You held it up, played it wide, the cross comes in, you head it. Right now a chance is picked from your position and where the ball is, with no memory of what just happened.
  • Difficulty settings, with a mode you have to earn
    Easy is roughly original NSS — five goals a game from day one. Hard makes training brutal (five drills for +1) and the opposition far stronger. Win the Ballon d'Or on Hard and something harder again unlocks.
    Why this came up
    • It answers the split in the room: one of us finds easy scoring boring, and a casual player bounces off a game that is hard from minute one.
    • Named as being like FM's challenge modes rather than a difficulty slider.
  • Keepers that get better as you climb
    From playing real NSS for research: early keepers only go to ground, and a bouncing ball straight at them can go over. Higher up they start reaching top corners.
    What it buys
    • Scoring is easy at the start because the keeper is bad, not because the game is generous — and you can feel the difference when you move up.
    • Same idea for everything around you: your energy drink does 10% in the bottom division and a lot more once you have a real gym behind you.
  • Unlocks tied to star rating and to reaching a new league
    New mechanics arrive as you climb, so a promotion changes what the game is, not just the badge next to your name. Compared to Spore and Blue Lock on the call.
  • Achievements paying out coins
    Something to spend progress on beyond the shop.
  • Tap a team-mate to pass, and play carries on
    From the five-a-side build. Tap him, he goes, he can lay it back to you — the move does not cut away the moment you release.
  • A ball that is already moving when it reaches you
    A cross should arrive travelling. Right now the ball you strike is sitting still even when it was just played into you.
  • Take a touch round the keeper
    Rather than only shooting from where the chance starts.
  • Left and right mid should probably be wingers
    Raised as a real doubt about the position list rather than a decision.
  • The goal and the six-yard box might be the wrong size disagreed
    Mikey: the goal and six-yard box look too small, and a tight angle does not look like a tight angle. Harry: the pitch is deliberately bigger than life because it is cartoony. Unresolved — worth measuring rather than arguing.
  • The tight-angle chance is too easy
    “I could score every time.” The keeper is not on his line where he should be, and a tight angle in real football is a hard chance, not an easy one.
  • Goalie mode needs a reason to be in a player's career question
    “What is goalie mode doing in my career as a player?” Liked as a thing to play, questioned as a thing that belongs here.
  • The engine should run in the trial and in training too disagreed
    Mikey's view is that the opening is the hook and matters as much as anything. Harry's is that scenarios come first. Both agreed it is near the top either way.
  • Scale the whole game to the player, because we actually can
    NSS did not scale its world because it could not. Playing Liverpool could mean genuinely worse team-mates around you, not just a higher number on the opposition.

Still open from before the call

  • The infinite match is not built yet
    The tool for seeing what a real match actually serves you, over thousands of minutes. Looked for on the call and not there.
  • One-on-ones are showing up in game again
    Confirmed live on the call — “finally”. Worth saying plainly that this was confirmed by playing, not by a test.
  • Only one-on-one has drawings
    Tight angle and free kick have one each, which is below the five needed for a rule. Eleven kinds have none and run on the generator's built-in shapes.
