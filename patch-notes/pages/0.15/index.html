Changed · The four headlines

Problem: in every match you play, your club has played like a 60-rated side

Whatever club you're at you win about 4 in 10, top or bottom of the table. That's why a max-stat Chelsea striker went 20+ games without a penalty.

Why, and the fix
  • Why: the unseen part of the match was told your relationship with team-mates (60 for everyone) instead of your club's strength (Chelsea: 83). It has been like that in every match, every career, as far back as the history goes. Matches you don't play used real strengths, so the rest of the table was right.
  • Fix: tell the match your club's real strength. Team-mate relationship keeps its real job, how well team-mates combine inside a chance. It goes in first because it changes every other number.
  • Strongest clubs win 38–49% → 71–78%; middle 38–43% → 49–57%; weakest 34–42% → 30–36%.
Problem: a rule nobody asked for moved every drawn keeper at least 1.6 m off his line

You drew all 10 penalty keepers on the line; the real match started him 1.6 m out every time (200 of 200). It also moved every cutback keeper and 44% of tight-angle keepers.

Why, and the fix
  • Why: one line added on 21 Sep, while all the drawings were one-on-ones, went into the shared step that places every drawn keeper. Nothing was measured. As more kinds got drawings it moved all of them, and it had been writing itself into saved drawings.
  • Fix: delete it. Each kind's keeper comes from its own drawings; penalties come from the penalty rules.
  • Correction: an earlier check said the penalty keeper wasn't off his line. That was wrong; the check didn't go through the real match.
  • A search found 11 rules sitting between your drawings and the screen: you asked for 3, one is a missing line, seven nobody asked for.
  • Free kicks: an ordinary taker scored 18% with him 1.6 m out against 6% on his line; your target is 4–7%.
Problem: the keeper moves differently in every kind of chance, and a 45 and an 88 are almost the same

On your shots he stands still until the ball arrives (he moves early in 1% of one-on-ones); on penalties he dives, then dives back.

One-on-ones conceded vs an 88 keeper (%)
28% 17%
Why, and the fix
  • Why: several separate keeper rulebooks, one per situation, each hand-tuned.
  • Fix: one keeper who gets set while you aim, reacts in 0.29 s (45 rated) to 0.21 s (90), reads where the ball will cross, dives once and never turns round. Built from your idea.
  • He moves before the ball arrives in 71–93% of one-on-ones, up from 1%.
  • He deliberately does not read your arrow: that turns aiming into a feint game.
Problem: highlights look alike: 85% of tight angles look like one from your last 20

Long shots are a straight copy of one drawing every time, and a striker gets 1.8 one-on-ones a match.

Tight angles like one of your last 5 (%)
51% 13%
Why, and the fix
  • Why: 68% of highlights are one of your drawings nudged up to 2 m, your 12 tight angles sit within about 2 m of each other, and the memory of recent pictures is wiped every match.
  • Fix: both ways built and switchable. (A) your drawings, cleaned up and mirrored half the time. (B) a true generator built from the spread of all your drawings.
  • Straight copies of a drawing: tight angles 31% → 2%, one-on-ones 48% → 7%, long shots 100% → 0% with (B).

Changed · The rest of the plan

Penalties

Goal drawn where the real one is, hard penalty rules nobody can edit, a run-up and 1-second timer, one dive, 99 power capped near 102 km/h, a reputation hit for a cheeky miss.

Shootouts and penalties won without you

Every kick played live on the real pitch, a Shootout button in the highlights, and penalties won at about one every 6 games for a top side.

The keeper's pieces

A dive that shows why he was beaten, and his lean set from where you drew him (8 of 10 dives leaned the wrong way first).

Around the shot

A team-mate no longer takes your shot, you can shoot past a man, team-mates curl round one, and a block can go in.

Camera and the drag

The whole goal always on screen, byline crosses filmed from the corner flag, room near the side, a shorter drag, and captain runs that start when you pull back.

Subs, ratings and energy

Late subs get chances, a sub isn't taken off again, a ratings list, and a can button that fits kick-off.

Match day and the season

Added time and Fergie time, a cup tab that names the right round, no draw after you're out, live scores, and a Sim this match button.

Screens and tools

Swipe pages for Stats, Home and Training, real League One and Two players, and a Match Radar to watch the unseen 90 minutes.
